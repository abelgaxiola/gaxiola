Gaxiola is a rare surname of Basque origin that became firmly established in northwestern Mexico during the Spanish colonial period. Today the overwhelming majority of people carrying it live in Sinaloa, Sonora, and Baja California, with a substantial secondary population in Arizona, California, and Texas. It belongs to the broad family of Basque toponymic surnames — alongside Arriola, Loyola, Echeverría, Elizondo, and Gastélum — names that identified a family by the land or farmstead it held.

There is no universal Gaxiola coat of arms, despite what commercial heraldry vendors advertise. What the surname does have is an unusually deep documentary record — parish registers in colonial Sinaloa reach back to the first decade of the 1700s — and a long association with families in ranching, agriculture, law, politics, education, sport, and public service.
